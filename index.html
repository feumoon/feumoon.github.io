CAST

CAST began from a moment when I was afraid to make a painting.

I looked at the blank paper and did not know where to begin. I was afraid to make the first mark.

Then I thought of Simon Peter, who had spent the night casting his nets without catching anything. When Jesus told him to go into the deep and let down his nets again, Peter chose to trust His word and cast the net once more.

I wanted to do the same.

I threw ink onto the paper.

For me, casting is an act of courage — the courage to cast hope once again into the deepest parts of life, and the courage to take one more step toward the world despite fear.

But the work does not end with the act of casting.

Once the ink is released, it begins to move on its own. It flows, spreads, gathers, and leaves traces beyond my complete control. I look at what has emerged and respond to it — sometimes following its movement, sometimes interrupting it, and sometimes adding another layer.

The work becomes a relationship between the artist and the flowing material — between what is chosen and what is allowed to happen.

My hand does not simply control the image. It listens to what the material has already made and decides where to intervene.

Each layer accumulates upon the previous one, becoming a trace of time.

The layers become like a life.

We make a choice, leave a trace, and live through the time that follows. Then another moment arrives, and another trace is added. What we become is formed through these accumulated moments.

CAST is therefore both an act of beginning again and a record of time — of courage, hope, and the layers of a life that continue to accumulate.
