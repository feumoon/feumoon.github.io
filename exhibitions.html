ABOUT THE EXHIBITION

MoonLight in the Beginning began with a rusted screw found in the yard of the artist's parents' factory. From this small trace of industrial life, the exhibition reflects on the relationship between individuals and the structures of contemporary society.

READ MORE +

The artist's work began with a single screw found in her parents' factory. A rusted screw, thrown away in the yard, reminded her of herself. This small, discarded object became a starting point for reflecting on her own life and the lives of people living in contemporary society.

After graduating, the artist temporarily put aside her desire to live as an artist and experienced society through working life. There, she encountered a gap between the individual and the structures of the city.

The exhibition brings together the industrial landscape of the city with the natural image of shimmering light on the sea. The reflection of Yoonseul fills the exhibition space and enters the screens, allowing the colors of nature to seep into the urban environment.

In the work, Yoonseul speaks of our beginning — a pure and beautiful world. Against this image, the exhibition asks what kind of lives we are living within the world we have built.

The numerous screens and human eyes represent an increasingly verticalized industrial and competitive society. The eyes seem to watch one another, creating a feeling of surveillance and tension. Through their movement, the inorganic monitors begin to resemble living organisms.

The work reflects on society and the individual, the individual and the community, and the possibility of a more humane world.

Yoonseul is a Korean word meaning the shimmering ripples of sunlight or moonlight reflected on water.
